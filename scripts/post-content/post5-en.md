# How to Open a Business Bank Account: A Practical No-Nonsense Guide

**Mixing personal money with business money is the most expensive mistake an entrepreneur makes.** If your LLC faces a lawsuit, a judge can "pierce the corporate veil" and hold you personally liable for business debts. Your home, your car, your personal savings — all exposed. The separation starts with a business bank account.

---

## Why You Need a Separate Bank Account for Your Business

A business bank account isn't a luxury — it's a legal and practical necessity. Here's why:

### Legal Protection of Your LLC

The main reason to form an LLC is to limit your personal liability. But if you mix personal and business funds, a judge can determine your LLC is a "fiction" and deny you that protection. This is called "piercing the corporate veil."

### Tax Separation

The IRS expects your business to have clear financial records. If you mix funds, your accountant will spend more time (and you'll pay more) separating personal from business expenses. In an audit, this can result in penalties for inadequate records.

### Professionalism with Clients and Vendors

When you issue payments from a business account, your clients see your business name. This projects seriousness and professionalism. Additionally, many B2B vendors only accept payments from business accounts.

### Access to Business Financial Products

Business accounts give you access to:
- Business lines of credit
- Business credit cards (with higher limits)
- Business loans (more favorable rates than personal loans)
- Payroll services
- Merchant services to accept card payments

## Documents You Need to Open Your Account

### For a Single-Member LLC

1. **EIN (Employer Identification Number)** from the IRS — CP 575 document or 147C letter
2. **Articles of Organization** approved by the Secretary of State
3. **Operating Agreement** (even if single-member)
4. **Government-issued ID** of the owner (passport, driver's license, or ITIN card)
5. **Proof of business address** (bank statement, lease agreement, or utility bill)

### For a Multi-Member LLC

Everything above plus:

6. **Signed Operating Agreement** with ownership distribution detailed
7. **LLC Resolution** authorizing account opening and designating who can sign
8. **Government IDs** of all members with signing authority

### If You're a Sole Proprietorship

1. **EIN or SSN** (you can use your SSN if you don't have an EIN, but it's better to get the EIN)
2. **DBA (Doing Business As)** registered if using a trade name
3. **Government ID** of the owner
4. **Proof of address**

## How to Choose the Right Bank for Your Business

### Factors to Consider

1. **Monthly fees** — Look for $0-$15 monthly. Some banks waive fees if you maintain a minimum balance.

2. **Minimum balance** — If you're starting out, look for accounts with no minimum balance or low minimums ($0-$100).

3. **Cash access** — Do you need to make cash deposits frequently? Traditional banks make this easier. Online banks may charge for cash deposits.

4. **Integration with accounting software** — If you use QuickBooks, Wave, or Xero, choose a bank that integrates natively to save time on reconciliation.

5. **Future credit lines** — If you anticipate needing financing, a traditional bank gives you history to apply for business loans.

6. **Bilingual service** — If you prefer Spanish-language service, traditional banks with local branches are your best option.

### My Recommendation for Most People

**If you're starting with little capital:**
- **Main account:** Mercury or Relay (no fees, no minimum balance, excellent technology)
- **Tax savings account:** Novo or Relay (separate 25-30% of each income for taxes)

**If you have consistent income ($5K+ monthly):**
- **Main account:** First Horizon or Regions (local bank with personal relationships)
- **Secondary account:** Mercury or Relay (for online operations and e-commerce)

**If you expect rapid growth or seek investors:**
- **Main account:** Bank of America or Truist (national network, access to advanced business services)

## Step by Step: How to Open Your Business Account

### Step 1: Get Your EIN

If you don't have it, apply for free on the IRS website. You need your SSN or ITIN, and the process is immediate online.

### Step 2: Gather All Documents

Have ready:
- IRS CP 575 letter (confirms your EIN)
- Articles of Organization (Secretary of State certificate)
- Signed Operating Agreement
- Two government IDs (passport + driver's license, for example)
- Proof of address (personal bank statement or utility bill)

### Step 3: Compare Banks and Choose

Use the criteria above to decide. You can open accounts online (Mercury, Relay) or visit branches (First Horizon, Regions, Bank of America).

### Step 4: Complete the Application

**Online:** Fill out digital forms, upload documents, verify identity. Time: 15-30 minutes.

**In branch:** Bring original documents. The banker helps you with the process. Time: 30-60 minutes.

### Step 5: Wait for Approval

**Online banks:** Approval in 1-3 business days.
**Traditional banks:** Can be immediate (branches) or 3-5 business days (manual verification).

### Step 6: Deposit Initial Funds

Transfer from your personal account to the business account. This is recorded as "initial capital" or "owner's equity contribution."

### Step 7: Set Up Tools

- Connect to QuickBooks, Wave, or your accounting software
- Set up low balance alerts
- Add users with limited permissions (if you have partners or employees)

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

### 1. Continuing to Use Your Personal Account

**Consequence:** You lose LLC legal protection. If there's a lawsuit, the judge can hold you personally liable.

**Solution:** Open the business account from day one. Transfer all business money to that account.

### 2. Not Separating Tax Money

**Consequence:** When it's time to pay quarterly taxes, you don't have the money. You end up taking loans or paying late payment penalties.

**Solution:** Open a second business account (sub-account) exclusively for taxes. Transfer 25-30% of each income immediately.

### 3. Making Informal "Loans" to the Business

**Consequence:** If you lend money to your business without documentation, the IRS can reclassify it as income or deny deductions.

**Solution:** If you lend money to the business, document it with a formal loan agreement (promissory note) with interest rate and payment schedule.

### 4. Not Reconciling Bank Statements

**Consequence:** Errors, duplicate transactions, or fraud go unnoticed. Your accounting books don't reflect reality.

**Solution:** Reconcile your business account every month. Compare your accounting software with the bank statement.

### 5. Paying Personal Expenses from the Business Account

**Consequence:** Record contamination. Your accountant doesn't know which expenses are deductible. In audit, the IRS can deny all deductions.

**Solution:** Never pay personal expenses from the business account. If you need personal money, make a "distribution" (transfer to your personal account) and pay from there.

### 6. Not Maintaining Required Minimum Balance

**Consequence:** If your account requires a $500 minimum balance and falls below, they charge monthly fees ($15-$35).

**Solution:** Read your account terms. If you can't maintain the minimum, switch to an account with no requirements.

## What a Business Bank Account Actually Costs

### Typical Fees

| Item | Typical Range |
|------|-------------|
| Monthly fee | $0-$29.95 |
| Cash deposit fee | $0-$5 (after certain limit) |
| Wire transfer fee | $15-$30 |
| Check issued fee | $0-$0.50 |
| Low balance fee | $0-$35 |
| Account closure fee | $0-$50 |

### How to Minimize Costs

- **Choose no-fee monthly accounts** — Mercury, Relay, Novo don't charge.
- **Maintain minimum balance if applicable** — If your bank charges $15 but waives the fee with $1,000 balance, maintain that balance.
- **Limit cash deposits** — If your bank charges for cash deposits, use your bank's ATMs or partner banks.
- **Negotiate fees** — If you have good history, some traditional banks reduce fees.

**My recommendation:** Start with free accounts (Mercury/Relay). If your business grows and you need advanced banking services, migrate to a traditional bank.
