# Restaurant Accounting: The Complete Guide Every Hispanic Owner Needs

**60% of restaurants close within the first three years. Most don't fail because of bad food, but because of bad accounting.** If you own a restaurant in the United States, understanding the numbers is not optional. It's the difference between surviving and closing.

---

## Why Restaurant Finances Fail

Restaurants have unique financial dynamics that make them especially vulnerable:

- **Extremely low profit margins** (3-5% net on average)
- **Daily cash flow** hard to track
- **Perishable inventory** that is lost if not managed properly
- **Complex labor costs** with tips, overtime, and regulations
- **Multiple payment methods** (cash, cards, delivery apps)

A restaurant can be packed every night and still lose money if the numbers are not controlled.

## The 5 Numbers Every Owner Must Know Weekly

### 1. Cost of Goods Sold (COGS)

**Formula:** (Beginning Inventory + Purchases - Ending Inventory) / Total Sales

**Target:** 28-32% of total sales. If it exceeds 35%, you have inventory leakage, incorrect portions, or theft.

### 2. Labor Cost

**Formula:** (Wages + Taxes + Benefits) / Total Sales

**Target:** 25-30% of sales. Includes everything: cooks, servers, dishwashers, employee insurance, FICA.

### 3. Gross Margin

**Formula:** Sales - COGS - Labor

Must be at least 40-45% to cover rent, utilities, and other fixed expenses.

### 4. Break-Even Point

**Formula:** Fixed Costs / (1 - (COGS% + Labor%))

This is how much you need to sell each month just to not lose money.

### 5. Prime Cost

**Formula:** COGS + Total Labor

**Target:** No more than 60% of total sales. This is the most important number.

## Inventory Control: The Method That Works

### FIFO System (First In, First Out)

Everything that arrives first is used first. Organize your storage so older products are in front.

### Daily Protein Count

Meat, fish, chicken — these are your most expensive items. Count them every day at closing. If 2 pounds of meat are missing and there's no sales record, you have a problem.

### Standardized Recipes (Recipe Cards)

Every dish must have an exact recipe with measured quantities:

- 4 oz of chicken (not "a little")
- 2 tablespoons of oil
- 1/2 cup of rice

This gives you:
1. **Exact cost per dish** for correct pricing
2. **Consistency** for the customer
3. **Control** against waste or theft

### Waste Report

Every time you throw something away, record it: what product, what quantity, why (expired, dropped, burned). At the end of the week, add up the total cost. If it's more than 2-3% of COGS, you have an operational problem.

## Tips and Employee Management

### Correct Tip Reporting

Tips are income for the employee, but you have obligations:

- **Report all tips to the IRS** (Form 8027 for large restaurants)
- **Withhold taxes** on reported tips
- **Include tips in minimum wage calculation** if applicable

### Employee Classification

- **Tipped:** Servers, bartenders, hosts who receive tips
- **Non-tipped:** Cooks, dishwashers, managers

Each group has different minimum wage and overtime rules.

### Overtime

Any hours over 40 per week = 1.5x regular salary. Many restaurants make the mistake of paying everything as a fixed salary without calculating overtime, and the IRS charges them retroactively with interest.

## Monthly Reports You Need

### 1. Profit & Loss (P&L)
Sales - COGS - Labor - Rent - Other expenses = Profit or Loss

### 2. Cash Flow
How much money came in vs went out. Restaurants need daily positive cash flow.

### 3. Balance Sheet
Assets (cash, inventory, equipment) vs Liabilities (debts, accounts payable)

### 4. COGS Report by Category
- Proteins
- Vegetables
- Beverages
- Dairy
- Dry/Canned goods

This tells you where your money is going.

### 5. Sales Report by Hour and Day
To schedule staff correctly and avoid paying for employees you don't need.

## Restaurant-Specific Taxes

### Sales Tax

You collect sales tax from the customer and pay it to the state. It's not your money — it's state money that you have temporarily. If you spend it, you'll have to pay it out of your pocket.

### Payroll Tax

- **Social Security & Medicare (FICA):** 7.65% that you pay as employer
- **FUTA (Federal Unemployment):** 0.6% on first $7,000 of each employee
- **State Unemployment:** Varies by state

### Income Tax Withholding

You withhold from each employee's salary based on their W-4.

### Equipment Depreciation

Refrigerators, stoves, tables — depreciate over 5-7 years. This legally reduces your taxable income.

## Mistakes That Cost Thousands of Dollars

1. **Not separating personal and business finances** — If you pay your personal rent with the restaurant account, you have no way of knowing if your business is profitable.

2. **Pricing without calculating real cost** — If you don't know a dish costs you $4.50 in ingredients and you sell it for $12, you might be losing money when you consider labor and expenses.

3. **Not recording cash sales** — The IRS can audit and estimate your sales based on inventory purchases. If you didn't record everything, they'll charge you taxes on money you never earned.

4. **Paying employees "under the table"** — If an employee gets hurt and isn't on payroll, you're 100% responsible. The fines are devastating.

5. **Not doing daily bank reconciliation** — If you don't verify that what came in on cards matches your reported sales, you won't know if there's fraud or errors.
